BACKATIT - USER GUIDE
=====================

Reminds you to get up and walk while you work, then tells you when to come
back.


FILES
-----
The program itself is installed together, in one directory:

  /usr/lib/backatit/          from the .deb
  ~/.local/lib/backatit/      from ./install.sh
  /usr/local/lib/backatit/    from sudo ./install.sh --system

  alarm.sh      the launcher (this is what the icon opens)
  settings.py   the settings window
  alarmd.sh     the background app that does the timing
  alert.py      the full-screen alert window
  alertctl.sh   shows one alert on demand (the Test buttons)
  lib.sh        shared code

Your own files live in the standard per-user places, and installing,
upgrading or removing the program never touches them:

  ~/.config/backatit/config              your settings
  ~/.local/state/backatit/stats.tsv      your statistics

Runtime state lives in $XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/backatit/ (usually
/run/user/1000/backatit/) and is wiped when you log out, so nothing can get
"stuck" between sessions.


HOW A CYCLE WORKS
-----------------
1. You work for the break interval (default 30 minutes).
2. Optionally, a notification warns you a couple of minutes ahead.
3. "Take a break!" appears full-screen with the alarm sounding.
4. You click "I took my break!" and go for a walk.
5. After the walk length (default 1 minute) "Walk time is up" appears and
   keeps nagging until you click "I'm back at work".
6. The next work block starts.

Step 5 is optional - untick "Tell me when the walk is over".

The countdown restarts when you ANSWER, not when the alert appeared. However
long an alert sits on screen, you still get a full work block afterwards.


THE ALERT WINDOW
----------------
It covers the whole screen, stays on top, and cannot be closed by Escape or
Alt+F4 - only by clicking a button.

The keyboard is ignored completely. Nothing you type can dismiss it. This is
deliberate: the old version used a normal dialog, and a keystroke landing on
the focused button killed the alarm the instant it appeared - which is exactly
what happens when an alarm interrupts you mid-sentence.

On top of that, any button that would cost you something stays greyed out for
3 seconds, with a small "buttons active in 3..." countdown, so a click already
on its way cannot hit it either. That covers "I took my break!", "Snooze" and
"Turn off reminders". "I'm back at work" and the hold-to-unlock bar respond
immediately - a stray click on those costs nothing, and the unlock bar needs a
sustained hold anyway.

Buttons on the break alert:
  I took my break!     dismiss and start the walk
  Snooze N min         ask again shortly
  Turn off reminders   see below - deliberately small and out of the way

Buttons on the back-to-work alert:
  I'm back at work     dismiss, next work block begins
  Turn off reminders

"Turn off reminders" does not destroy your setup. It clears the manual switch,
and if a schedule is running it mutes it for the REST OF TODAY only. Tomorrow
runs as configured, and "Start reminders" in the settings window starts it
again immediately.


WHAT THE ALERTS SAY
-------------------
The heading and the message on both alerts are yours to change, on the Alerts
tab. Press "Test break alert" to see your wording on the real thing.

A message can run to more than one line: type a line break in the box and it
appears in the alert. Anything is safe to type - quotes, apostrophes, brackets
and symbols are all treated as plain text.

Leave a heading empty and it falls back to the built-in wording rather than
showing you a blank alert.


SOUND
-----
The break alarm repeats until you answer, then goes quiet after 90 seconds
while the window stays put.

The back-to-work alarm never gives up. It buzzes for a minute, falls silent
for 2 minutes, buzzes again, waits 4 minutes, then 8, and so on - each silence
twice as long as the last, up to the cap you set ("Nag me at most every ...
minutes", default 15). So it is still going when you sit back down, without
blaring non-stop the whole time you are away.

The two alerts use different sounds so you can tell them apart from another
room.


SETTINGS
--------
Open "BackAtIt" (desktop icon, or search in Activities).

THE PANEL AT THE TOP tells you what is happening right now:

    Running                                        13m 57s
    Next break in
    [ Stop reminders ]   Today: 3 taken . 1 snoozed . 0 skipped

The big number counts down live, so you can tell at a glance that the app is
actually running. It shows either the time until your next break, or - during
a walk - the time left of the walk. It reads "Alert on screen" while an alert
is waiting for you, and "Not running" if the background app is stopped.

THE BUTTON beside it is the on/off switch:

  Start reminders   begin now, and clear any "off for today"
  Stop reminders    stop now (a running schedule is muted for the rest of
                    today, and resumes tomorrow)

Start also saves whatever you have changed, so it always runs with the
numbers you can see - the background app works from the config file, so an
unsaved interval would otherwise be ignored. Stop does not save: an unsaved
edit stays unsaved, and the "Unsaved changes" note stays up.

Both take effect immediately. The button changes the moment you press it, and
the background app is nudged to notice at once rather than at its next check.

THE WINDOW has the tabs at the top left and the live state at the top right:

    [ Timing ][ When it runs ][ Alerts ][ Stats ][ Guide ]    * 17m 19s [Stop]

The counter answers "is this actually on?" at a glance - if it is ticking
down, a break is coming. A dash means nothing is counting. Hover the counter
and a tooltip spells the state out in words: running, alert on screen, off for
today, or not running. The button beside it starts and stops the reminder.

THE TABS hold everything else, so only one group is on screen at a time:

  Timing         how long you work, the walk, and the warning beforehand
  When it runs   the daily schedule, days off, away detection, login start
  Alerts         the two sounds and how the alert window behaves
  Stats          how many breaks you have actually taken
  Guide          how the whole thing works, and how to set it up

Each group is a card. Every setting shows its name above the field, the field
itself across the full width of the card, and a line underneath saying what it
does. Anything belonging to a switched-off option is greyed out - name, field
and explanation together - so a tab only offers what is actually in effect.

Inside a tab the cards rearrange to fit the window: widen it and they form two
columns, wider still and three; narrow it and they stack into one and scroll.

  under 1000px    one column
  1000 to 1456    two columns
  1456 and up     three columns

The window takes its colours from your desktop theme, so it matches whether
you run a light or a dark one.


STATS
-----
The Stats tab charts the last 7 or 30 days: one row per day, with the bar
running left to right and split into breaks taken (green), snoozed (amber),
and skipped because you were already away (grey). The number on the right is
the day's total; hover a row for the exact breakdown.

The rows are laid out horizontally so the chart is the same width whichever
period you pick - switching to 30 days makes the list longer, not the window
wider. The bars stretch to fill the column, so widening the window makes them
longer and easier to compare. A day with no breaks still shows an empty track,
so it reads as a genuine zero rather than as missing data.

Underneath is the total for the period and how many breaks you averaged on the
days you were actually at the desk - days you were away do not drag the
average down.

WHERE THE FIGURES LIVE

  ~/.local/state/backatit/stats.tsv

One line per event, "date<tab>event". It survives logging out and is not
touched by anything else in the app. The path is also shown on the Stats tab.

CLEARING IT: the "Clear history" button on the Stats tab empties the file and
every day goes back to zero. A copy is kept alongside as stats.tsv.bak, so it
is recoverable if you press it by mistake.

To see what a full history looks like without waiting a month:

  ./demo-stats.sh on       swap in 30 days of made-up figures
  ./demo-stats.sh off      put your real ones back
  ./demo-stats.sh status   say which is in place

"on" copies your real file to stats.tsv.real first and will not overwrite that
copy, so running it twice cannot lose anything.


  How often
    Break every ... minutes     length of a work block, default 30
    Snooze length ... minutes   how long Snooze postpones, default 5

  After the break
    Tell me when the walk is over   turn the second stage on or off
    Break length ... minutes        how long the break itself is, default 1
    Nag me at most every ... min    cap on the back-to-work silence, default 15

  Before the break
    Warn me that a break is coming  heads-up notification
    Warn me this far ahead          how much notice, default 2 minutes

  When you are away
    Skip the break if I am already away   see AWAY DETECTION below
    Count me away after ... minutes       default 5

  Daily schedule
    Run automatically between set hours   see SCHEDULE below
    Between these hours                   24-hour clock, like 09:00 and 18:30
    Days off                              switch on any days to skip

  Alerts
    Break heading / Break message      what the "get up" alert says
    Back-to-work heading / message     what the "come back" alert says
    Break sound / Back-to-work sound   any .oga / .wav / .mp3 / .flac
                                       (the small play button tries one out)
    Keep the break alarm sounding      repeat until you answer
    Full-screen alerts                 cover the screen; leave this on
    Test break alert                   show the real alert right now
    Test back-to-work alert            show the nagging alert right now

  Startup
    Start automatically when I log in  keep this on if you use the schedule
    Quit background app                stop everything until you open this
                                       window again

The name sits at the top left of the window, the tabs under it, and the
counter and Start/Stop button at the top right.

At the bottom left the window tells you where it stands:

  Unsaved changes     you have altered something that is not written yet
  * Saved at 19:11    it has just been written; this clears itself shortly

At the bottom right:

  Close            shuts the window. If anything is unsaved it asks first,
                   offering Back, Discard, or Save and close.
  Save             writes your changes and leaves the window open
  Save and close   writes them and shuts the window in one go

If a schedule time is not a valid clock time, saving says so and keeps the old
one - and "Save and close" leaves the window open rather than closing over the
top of the complaint.


BREAK LENGTH
------------
Every "... minutes" box accepts fractions, in steps of half a minute:

  0.5  =  30 seconds
  1.5  =  90 seconds
  30   =  30 minutes  (default)

The allowed range is 0.5 to 240 minutes. When the interval is under two
minutes the app shows the next break time with seconds (19:55:21).


LOCKING THE SCREEN FOR THE WHOLE BREAK
--------------------------------------
Timing -> "Lock the screen for the whole break" (on by default) turns the
alert from something you dismiss into something you wait out. Untick it for
an alert you answer with "I took my break!" and that then gets out of the way.

With it on there is no "I took my break!" to press. The screen is locked the
moment the alarm sounds and the break starts counting down there and then, so
there is no gap in which you could quietly sit back down. Your other monitors
go dark too, unless you untick "Cover every monitor".

While the break runs the window shows how long is left and a bar filling as it
goes. The mouse and keyboard belong to the alert, so Super and Alt-Tab stop
working. Clicking still works normally - the lock points input AT the alert,
it does not switch your mouse off.

Two ways out:

  Snooze              one click. Abandons the break and puts the next one off
                      by your snooze length. Counted as a snooze.
  Turn off reminders  stops reminders for the rest of today, as everywhere
                      else in the app.
  Hold to unlock      hold the button down for five seconds and the machine is
                      yours again. This does NOT end your day - the next break
                      arrives exactly when it would have. It is recorded as a
                      break cut short, so the tally stays honest.

When the break time is up the mouse and keyboard are released and the alert
becomes the ordinary back-to-work one, buzzing until you answer it.

There is also a safety timer. If the app ever froze mid-break it would let go
of your mouse and keyboard anyway, rather than leave you stuck - the
hold-to-unlock button only helps while the app is still responding.

This needs the walk stage: the lock ends when the walk is up, so with "Tell me
when the walk is over" unticked there is nothing for it to end at, and both
tick boxes grey out.

Press "Test break alert" on the Alerts tab to see the real locked window with
a ten-second stand-in break.

It works best under X11 or XWayland, which is what the app asks for. In a
session with no X server at all the claim on the mouse and keyboard is
refused, and the lock falls back to what an ordinary alert does: covering the
screen and ignoring the keyboard.


AWAY DETECTION
--------------
With "Skip the break if I am already away" ticked, the app checks how long it
has been since you touched the mouse or keyboard. If that is longer than 5
minutes when a break falls due, you are clearly already having one, so the
alarm stays quiet and the timer simply starts again. It is counted as
"skipped" in today's tally.

Change the 5 minutes with "Count me away after" in the settings window.


SCHEDULE
--------
Tick "Run automatically between set hours" and set a start and end time.

Inside that window the reminder runs on its own - you do NOT have to open the
app, and you do not have to remember to switch it on. Outside the window it
stays quiet.

Windows that cross midnight work too, e.g. 22:00 to 02:00.

DAYS OFF is a row of seven buttons - switch on any combination you like, not
just the weekend. Fridays only, Monday/Wednesday/Friday, or nothing at all.
Leave them all off and the schedule runs every day.

For a window that crosses midnight, a shift belongs to the day it began: a
Friday-night shift runs through to Saturday morning even with Saturday off,
and Saturday's own shift is then skipped.

To turn it off, untick the box and press Save. "Start reminders" works
independently of the schedule: it runs the reminder even outside the
scheduled hours.

For the schedule to fire, the background app must be running, which is why
"Start automatically when I log in" should stay ticked.


TROUBLESHOOTING
---------------
Nothing happens at all
  Open the app and read the panel at the top. If the counter is ticking, it
  is running. If it says "Not running" or "Off", press "Start reminders".
  If it says "Off for the rest of today", "Start reminders" clears that too.

Check what the background app is doing:
  cat /run/user/1000/backatit/daemon.log

Force reset (only if something is genuinely stuck):
  pkill -f alarmd.sh
  rm -rf /run/user/1000/backatit
  Then open the app again.

No sound
  Test with:  pw-play /usr/share/sounds/freedesktop/stereo/alarm-clock-elapsed.oga
  Check the system volume is not muted.

The alert is not full screen
  Make sure "Full-screen alerts" is ticked. The alert is drawn by alert.py
  rather than by yad, because yad's own full-screen mode does not work on
  GNOME/Wayland here - it produced a small window instead.


INSTALLING IT ELSEWHERE
----------------------
This is a Linux desktop app. It needs:

  bash, python3, python3-gi with GTK 3   the window and the alert
  pw-play (pipewire) or paplay or aplay  the sound
  notify-send (libnotify)                the heads-up notifications
  gdbus (glib)                           away detection, GNOME only

It is written against the freedesktop desktop standards, so it should run on
GNOME, KDE, XFCE, Cinnamon and the rest. Two things are GNOME-specific and
degrade quietly elsewhere:

  - Away detection asks GNOME's idle monitor. Off GNOME it reads zero, which
    means "you are here", so breaks always fire and nothing is skipped.
  - The full-screen alert prefers XWayland so it can stay on top. Under other
    compositors it still goes full screen.

It will NOT run on Windows as it stands. The daemon is bash, sound goes
through PipeWire, away detection goes through D-Bus, and starting at login
uses ~/.config/autostart. Porting would mean rewriting those four pieces, not
repackaging.


NOTES
-----
Only one background app can run at a time; extra launches exit quietly, so
you cannot end up with two alarms firing at once.

The old WEEKDAYS_ONLY setting was replaced by DAYS_OFF (a list like "6,7").
An existing WEEKDAYS_ONLY=1 is carried over automatically the first time.

Settings not in the window can be changed in ~/.config/backatit/config:
  BUZZ_ON_MIN          how long each back-to-work buzz lasts (default 1)
  BUZZ_GAP_START_MIN   the first silence between buzzes (default 2)
  GRACE_SEC            how long the alert buttons stay greyed out (default 3;
                       0 turns the wait off entirely)
  LOCK_HOLD_SEC        how long the unlock button must be held (default 5)
  LOCK_ALARM_SEC       how long the alarm sounds on a locked break (default 10)
  LOCK_RELEASE_MARGIN  how long past the end of a locked break the input lock
                       gives up on its own, if the app has wedged (default 120)

Editing that file by hand is safe: the settings window changes values in
place and leaves your comments and any hand-set extras alone.
